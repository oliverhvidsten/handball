## Done

- **The draft: a lottery, a prospect class, and a live room with a clock** — `handball/draft_rules.py` (pure, DB-free, and it does not import sqlalchemy — a test asserts that) plus `handball/draft.py` (SQL) over alembic `0014`'s `draft_lotteries` / `draft_state` / `draft_prospects` and the new `draft_picks` columns, behind `POST /draft/{lottery,prospects,open,pick}` and the one `GET /draft/state` the room polls. The draft existed before this as an order seeded at rollover and an in-memory `DraftService` handing out a flat deal; neither knew who was on the board, whose turn it was, or what a pick was worth.
  - **The order is three different decisions.** Round 1 picks 1-16 belong to the LOTTERY, so the rollover seeds them with `pick_number` NULL — a number written at rollover is a number somebody could read before the draw. Picks 17-32 go to the playoff teams by how far they went (first-round losers, worse record first within each round, then the champion last), read off `playoff_series`. Round 2 ignores both the lottery and the bracket: 33-48 to the non-playoff teams worst-first, 49-64 to the playoff teams worst-first, which is the only reading of "no better than the 17th pick of the second round". Losing the lottery should not cost a team twice.
  - **The lottery is sequential and weight-positional**: weight *i* belongs to the *i*-th worst team still in the pool, the winner is removed, and the weights are renormalised over whoever is left — so `LOTTERY_WEIGHTS` states the odds on the FIRST pick only. The weights are read as shares and need not sum to anything, and a draw takes an injected `random.Random` with its **seed stored next to the result**: a draw nobody can replay is a draw nobody has to believe. Selection is an explicit cumulative walk rather than `random.choices`, so the historical record of a draw cannot move under the league's feet with a CPython release.
  - **Protections settle at the draw**, in the same transaction that numbers the picks. A top-*N* protected pick that lands at or inside *N* **reverts to the original team and the obligation ends** — no rollover to a later year, which would need a second pick to attach to and a second year to argue about. Inclusive on the boundary, because "top three protected" is what the two managers said to each other. The outcome is recorded (`conveyed` / `reverted`) so a pick that reverted still reads as a pick that was once traded. Attaching a protection at trade time is the Hall-of-Fame agent's half; this is only the resolution.
  - **Prospects are generated ONCE, at upload**, and the whole generated player is kept in `player_json` — generating at pick time would mean the board showed ratings the signed player did not have, and managers would be drafting a name. The file is parsed by `draft_simulator.load_prospect_names` (the offline generator's own parser, via a temp file, so there is one format and one place to fix it): one name per line, or a CSV with `Name` and an optional `Position`. The room will not open on fewer prospects than picks — a draft that runs out mid-round has to be unwound by hand.
  - **The room runs on the same lazy clock as free agency**, for the same reason: there is no scheduler in this deployment, so `GET /draft/state` sweeps an expired turn before it answers and the page polls that read. A turn older than `DRAFT_TURN_LIMIT_HOURS` (24h — shorter than free agency's 48, because a draft is one sitting) is taken automatically, best available by the rules, flagged `auto_pick` so the record says whether a person made the pick. At most ONE pick per read: an auto-pick starts the next team's clock at `now()`, so the team behind an absentee gets their full turn instead of inheriting somebody else's lateness.
  - **`draft_state` is the room's one gate.** Every pick takes that row `FOR UPDATE`, then team, then player — the league's existing lock order, rejoined — so two managers hammering the button serialize, and a manager who beats the clock by a second wins while the sweep finds the turn already moved on. The body of `POST /draft/pick` names a prospect, never a team: whose turn it is is a fact the league already knows, so the endpoint reads the team on the clock and demands `require_owns_strict` on THAT team. No commissioner bypass — a commissioner is also a manager with teams of their own, and picking for somebody else is the explicit `force`.
  - **Where you were taken IS the deal.** `ROOKIE_SCALE` prices every pick (5yr/$5M down to 2yr/$1M), which is what makes a traded pick a knowable asset — both managers can price the 14th pick without knowing who will be standing there. A pick outside the scale is an error, not a default. The in-memory `postseason.DraftService` now takes the same scale, so the offline stack and the live draft cannot disagree about what a pick costs.
  - **Undrafted prospects become ordinary unrestricted free agents** when the room closes. They are real players who were in the class and nobody took; the alternative is that they evaporate, which is both unkind and makes the size of the pool depend on how many picks got traded.
  - **Two phases now wait on it**: `POST /free-agency/periods` is refused until the draft is complete (a manager cannot know what holes they are signing to fill until they know which ones their picks filled), and a fourth `season_readiness` check (`draft_complete`) blocks period 1. Both treat a season with NO `draft_state` row as complete — leagues that predate the draft, and every offline fixture, must not be blocked by a phase they never had, which is also why a rollover with no bracket still seeds plain reverse standings and writes no draft at all.
  - `Draft.tsx` is the room: the order with holder, via-team, protections and what each pick paid; a sortable, filterable board with a Draft button on your turn; the countdown, and a banner naming any pick the poll itself made. `DraftPanel` walks the commissioner through one step at a time (draw → upload → open → force a pick) rather than showing four buttons three of which are about to be refused; it had to move out of the Commissioner page's Offseason block, which renders only for a season that has FINISHED — a draft belongs to the one the rollover has just opened.
  - Tested in `tests/test_draft_rules.py` (26 pure) and `tests/test_draft.py` (40 over Postgres: a rollover seeded off a real bracket, a full 64-pick draft to completion, protections reverting and conveying, the clock picking, undrafted prospects landing in the pool, and both gates), plus the rookie scale in `tests/test_postseason.py`.

- **Sealed offers were not sealed** — `GET /free-agency/state` served every rival's live offer during an open round. A board is created by the FIRST offer on a player (`_get_or_create_auction`), so it exists as `'collecting'` all through the sealed round, and the state read returned every live board with its offers on it. Any authenticated manager could read the whole market before deciding their own bid, which is the one thing a sealed round cannot allow. Both the endpoint docstring and the 0011 migration note asserted the opposite invariant ("an auction only exists once its round has closed") — the code had simply never matched them. Fixed by splitting `PUBLIC_AUCTION_STATUSES` off `LIVE_AUCTION_STATUSES`: a board is withheld entirely until its round closes, since even its existence says somebody bid on that player. A manager still sees their own offers, which arrive per-caller via `team_offers`. Pinned in `tests/test_free_agency.py` and from the API in `tests/test_api.py`.
- **An auto-forfeit clock** — `simulation_vars.FA_TURN_LIMIT_HOURS` (48h) plus `free_agency.sweep_expired_turns`. Sequential bidding is strictly ordered, so one manager who stops answering halts the board, the round, and the offseason; the only previous remedy was the commissioner noticing. There is no scheduler in this deployment, so the clock is LAZY: `GET /free-agency/state` sweeps before it reads, and the page polls that endpoint while a board is live — a stalled board cannot stay stalled while anyone is watching, and it needs no infrastructure. Expiry does exactly what the commissioner did by hand: forfeit the team on the clock, or decline an RFA match window. Never applied to `awaiting_award`, which waits on the commissioner — expiring the league's own turn would be nonsense. An expiry is logged with `by_commissioner=False` (a new parameter, separate from `forced`): the mechanics of an expiry and an intervention are identical, but only one of them is a person, and that is the most important thing the audit log records. Boards carry a server-computed `turn_deadline`/`turn_seconds_left` so the countdown doesn't drift with the viewer's clock.
- **A public "who bid what" record** — `free_agency.period_history` behind `GET /free-agency/history`, rendered as "Closed rounds" on the Free Agents page: every board that has resolved or been voided, with every offer ever made on it and how each one ended (signed / outbid / walked away / raised). The signings list said who won; this says what everyone else was willing to pay. Sealed rounds are excluded by board status, not by round, so it cannot leak the thing the fix above just closed.
- **Bulk contract administration** — `handball/contract_admin.py` behind `GET /contracts/audit` and `POST /contracts/bulk` (commissioner-only, dry run by default), with a Contracts panel on the Commissioner page that appears only when there is something to fix. The problem was worse than "half the league": **all 672 rostered players sat at `years_remaining <= 0`**, down to -18, because the counter was never initialised and `advance_year` has ticked it down every offseason since. `_process_free_agency` releases everyone at or below zero, so the next `/season/advance` would have emptied all 32 rosters.
  - The RESTART strategy is the repair: a counter that has run out restarts at the term already on the books. Term and value are untouched, so no payroll moves. OVERRIDES are explicit per-player (term, value) for the deals the commissioner wants to actually decide.
  - Every change is validated, whatever produced it — including a restart, which re-uses values from rows that *predate the contract rules* and so are exactly the ones that might not satisfy them. The plan is refused rather than half-applied; `apply_bulk` cannot fail on a plan that passed.
  - The cap rule is `assert_trade_hard_cap`, applied only to teams whose payroll actually moves. Six teams sit legally over the hard cap on rookie deals, and that function demands a strict *reduction* from a team already over — right for a trade, wrong for an untouched payroll. Skipping unchanged teams is what lets the repair run at all (the real league caught this).
  - It deliberately will not fix an over-cap team: which players to move is the team's call, and a commissioner tool that quietly rewrote salaries would be making it for them.
  - The plan reports what the next rollover would do before and after, which is the number the whole tool exists for. Tested pure in `tests/test_contract_admin.py` (28) and over Postgres in `tests/test_api.py`, including that a repaired league survives an actual rollover.
- **Roster-legality season-start check** — a third `season_readiness` check (`roster_legality`). A team that cannot be arranged into a legal lineup cannot play its games, and retirements and expiries both take players off a roster without asking whether what remains is legal. Reports a position it can't fill to starters+bench, too many bodies for the reserve bench, or — when the roster is otherwise fine — players sitting on it with no slot (a signing rebuilds the lineup best-effort, so one signed into an incomplete roster stays unplaced). Unplaced players are reported ONLY when the roster is arrangeable: while a position is short there is no legal lineup to be in, so naming both would send the manager after the wrong thing first. The check is pure over position COUNTS, which is enough to decide everything `canonical_team` decides without loading a Player, and it shares `DEFAULT_RULES` rather than duplicating the caps. As the registry promised, no API or UI change was needed. Against the live league it reports nothing — all 32 rosters are legal and fully placed.

- **One ranking rule for the whole league** — `handball/standings.py`. Points (3 for a win, 1 for a tie) → head-to-head → goal difference → goals for → team id. Everything that needs to know who finished where now reads it: the Standings page, the playoff seeding, and (reversed) the draft order.
  - It replaces a sort on wins-then-losses whose remaining ties fell out ALPHABETICALLY — survivable while it only set draft order, not once it seeds a bracket people look at. It never agreed with the `PTS` column the Standings page was already displaying either; a table that sorts differently from the bracket it seeds is worse than no table.
  - **Head-to-head is a mini-table**, applied recursively: among the tied teams only, count the points each earned against the others; if that splits {A,B,C} into {A} and {B,C}, then B vs C is re-decided by their own head-to-head rather than by whatever they did to A. Always available inside a conference — the schedule has every intra-conference pair playing 2-4 times.
  - W-L-T comes from `teams`; goals and head-to-head from that season's REGULAR-SEASON `games`, so a bracket can't seed itself off its own results.
  - `GET /standings` serves it (head-to-head cannot be expressed as an `ORDER BY`, so the page can no longer read Supabase directly) with each row's conference, division, division-leader flag, and projected playoff seed. `LeagueOperations` takes the rule as an injected `ranker`, since the full version needs a game log the orchestrator's repository doesn't carry; the offline stack falls back to the same POINTS key over the records it does have.
  - Tested DB-free in `tests/test_standings.py` (points, every tiebreak, circular ties, multi-way recursion) plus the endpoint in `tests/test_api.py`.
- **Playoffs** — `handball/playoffs.py` over alembic `0012`'s `playoff_series` (+ `0013`, which made each round a series), with the bracket's pure shape (seed, pair, name a round) staying in `handball/postseason.py` so the in-memory `PlayoffService` and the persisted one cannot disagree about what a bracket is. 8 per conference, quarterfinals → semifinals → conference final → a cross-conference Final.
  - **Every round is a BEST-OF-SEVEN** — first to 4, the higher seed hosting games 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 (2-2-1-1-1). A series stops the moment it is won, so a sweep is four games; a bracket runs ~70-90 games in all. The series score lives on the row (`high_wins`/`low_wins`) rather than being counted from the games, because that is what makes an interrupted round RESUMABLE: a worker that dies mid-series picks it up at 2-1 instead of replaying it. A drawn game goes to the higher seed, so a series always terminates whatever the engine's tie policy — production never reaches it, since `allow_tie=False` sends a level game to sudden-death overtime.
  - Worth knowing: **hosting confers no advantage today**. `game_simulator` opens with a fair coin flip and settles overtime with another, so home/away decides which side a game is recorded on and nothing else. If home advantage is ever wanted, the 2-2-1-1-1 pattern is already there to hang it on.
  - **A round per click.** `POST /playoffs/rounds/run` is a background job on the same run slot as a period (`season_state.run_kind` says which phase owns it, because `reset_run` rolls back by WEEK RANGE and would delete real games if it mistook one for the other). The next round's matchups are materialized the moment a round finishes, so managers see who they play and can fix a lineup before it is run. Teams are re-loaded for every GAME, so a lineup edit lands as soon as the round it precedes.
  - **Division-aware seeding.** Each conference's four DIVISION WINNERS take seeds 1-4, ordered among themselves; the best four remaining teams take 5-8. A division winner is seeded above a wildcard that finished ahead of it — winning a division is worth something, and that is the whole point of having divisions. It also means the 3 or 4 seed can genuinely be the underdog at home. The 1-4 / 5-8 split is derived from the division count, not hardcoded. Divisions are keyed `(conference, division)` via `league_structure.division_key`, because both conferences have a division named "South".
  - **Re-seeded, not a fixed ladder** — survivors are re-paired best-vs-worst each round on their ORIGINAL conference seed, so a 6-seed that upsets the 3 is still the 6. The Final is hosted by the better regular season, ranked by the same rule as everything else; both conference champions are usually 1-seeds, so the seed cannot break that tie, and the league table still holds the finished season because nothing in the postseason writes to `teams` and playoff goals are excluded.
  - **What a playoff game does not touch is the point.** Games are played on team copies loaded fresh and never saved (the trick the in-memory service already used), so W-L, the draft order, and the bracket's own seeding stay exactly as the regular season left them. What it does write: `games` + `player_game_lines` flagged `is_playoff`, so box scores and playoff stat lines exist while `player_season_stats` / `player_leaderboard` (recreated in 0012) and `offseason._top_player` see regular-season lines only — an award is for the season, and counting the postseason would hand every one of them to whoever went deepest.
  - **Injuries** carry over and tick per round, treating a round as a chunk (`PERIODS + round`) — but only for the teams that PLAYED it. An eliminated team's injuries carry to the offseason exactly as they would have if the playoffs had not happened.
  - `POST /playoffs/{start,rounds/run,reset}` are commissioner-only; `GET /playoffs/bracket` is the one document the Playoffs page renders and polls. `/season/advance` now waits for a champion, since the rollover zeroes the records the Final seeds itself from.
  - The conference/division map moved to `handball/league_structure.py` (`schedule_generator` re-exports it) — asking which conference a team is in should not import an OR-Tools solver.
  - Tested in `tests/test_playoffs.py` (Postgres, a whole bracket end to end) plus API authz/gating in `tests/test_api.py`; the pure seeding/pairing stays covered in `tests/test_postseason.py`.
- **Offseason free agency: sealed offers → RFA match → open auction** — the market, in `handball/free_agency_rules.py` (pure, DB-free, and it does not import sqlalchemy — a test asserts that) plus `handball/free_agency.py` (SQL) over alembic `0011`'s six tables. The commissioner opens a PERIOD; each ROUND takes sealed offers, then closes into per-player AUCTIONS: restricted players resolve by rights (own team led → stays; outside led → exclusive match window; declined → ordinary free agent with the offers already on the table), a sole offer signs itself, and two or more go to sequential bidding worst-initial-offer-first, where each turn is match / raise / forfeit. Last team standing signs at its own last offer; a full cycle with nobody raising goes to the commissioner. Rounds repeat until one draws no offers.
  - **Ranking** is one total order — salary, then term, then earliest submission (`fa_offers.id`, a bigserial, so it cannot tie the way a timestamp can). That tiebreak is what keeps MATCH and RAISE distinct: re-offering the leading contract ranks just below it.
  - **Exposure** (`check_exposure`) is the invariant the whole thing rests on: an offer is a promise, and a team may not promise more than it can keep. Bodies (one live offer per open roster spot), the hard cap, ONE mid-level exception shared across all outside offers, and — the case the sum alone misses — every offer must still be legal if it is the one that lands LAST, because the allowance falls off a cliff at each luxury threshold. A property test pins that an honourable board stays honourable as its offers convert.
  - **Offers are append-only**: an edit, a raise and a match all supersede rather than mutate, so the audit trail is honest and re-offering costs you your place in line.
  - **Concurrency**: lock order is period → round → auction → team → player, extending the team-then-player order `signing_service` already used. Managers take the round FOR SHARE and run in parallel; the commissioner's phase transitions take it FOR UPDATE and wait them out. Every path re-reads status after locking.
  - **Signings** all funnel through the extracted `signing_service.apply_signing`, so the pool deal and every auction award are the same write.
  - `POST /free-agency/{periods,offers,rounds/close,close}` and `/auctions/{id}/{bid,match,decline,force-forfeit,award}`, all behind `_require_owns_strict` (no commissioner bypass — in a sealed auction that would be a hole, not a convenience); `GET /free-agency/state` is the one document the page polls. Sealed offers are the only table not granted to `authenticated`: they are served through the API, which redacts per caller.
  - Tested in `tests/test_free_agency_rules.py` (68 pure) and `tests/test_free_agency.py` (Postgres, a whole period end to end), plus API authz/gating in `tests/test_api.py`.
