GAME MECHANICS RULES
Variables:
    Court length- 40 meters
    Goal area- 6 meters on each side
    Regulation- 60 minutes, two 30 minute halves

Every constant named below lives in simulation_vars.py. After changing any of them, run
`python scripts/calibrate_sim.py` -- it prints what the league actually does, and the
bands it should land in.

0. For each player, draw an offensive contribuition and a defensive contribution, sum to make total team offense
and defense for the game (weighted average for amount of time played). Before the game starts, generate a very large
list of numpy randoms, to be referenced throughout the game. Regenerate if needed.

1. Opening posession determined 50-50, ball starts at the 20

2. When a team has the ball, each pass is completed with probability PASS_COMPLETION; an
incomplete pass will lead to either a turnover or a loss of meters (could be 0).
offense / (offense + defense) sits at ~0.5 for every realistic matchup, so using it directly
as a completion rate would make every pass a coin flip -- which produced ~300 turnovers a
game. Instead that ratio is used only as an *edge* around parity and remapped in log-odds
space onto a believable band:

    edge            = offense / (offense + opposing_defense) - 0.5
    PASS_COMPLETION = sigmoid( logit(PASS_COMPLETION_BASE) + PASS_COMPLETION_GAIN * edge )
                      clamped to [PASS_COMPLETION_MIN, PASS_COMPLETION_MAX]

BASE sets the level (what evenly matched teams see) and GAIN sets the slope (what an
advantage is worth). Because the input is a ratio it is scale-free: when ratings inflate
across the league over seasons, completion -- and therefore scoring -- stays put.

3. If a pass is completed, distance travelled is normally distributed, pull distance from ~N(4, 1.5) for now, but should scale
depending on how much better the offense is than defense. Time spent will correlate with distance travelled.

4. When the ball is within shooting range, passes become shorter.

5. At any point, a player may feasibly take a shot. The chance of shooting rather than
passing is a sigmoid of court position, centred on SHOT_SIGMOID_MIDPOINT with steepness
SHOT_SIGMOID_STEEPNESS -- roughly 50% at the 6 meter line, and effectively certain at the
goal. See odds_of_taking_shot().

6. If a shot is taken, it is on goal with probability

    ON_GOAL_MAX * e^(-K * distance_from_goal) * shooter_skill

where shooter_skill is a modest multiplier around an average shooter,
1 + SHOOTER_SKILL_SLOPE * (shooter.offense - SHOOTER_SKILL_REF), clamped to
[SHOOTER_SKILL_MIN, SHOOTER_SKILL_MAX]. The skill term must be a multiplier, not the raw
0-10 rating -- a raw rating pushes this above 1.0 inside about 5 meters.

7. If a shot is on goal, it beats the keeper with a probability built from the same
log-odds remap as rule 2. The quality ratio weighs the attack (the team, plus a heavier
weight on the shooter) against the defense and the goalie:

    quality = (GOAL_TEAM_WEIGHT * offense + GOAL_SHOOTER_WEIGHT * shooter.offense)
              / (offense + defense + goalie)
    P(goal) = sigmoid( logit(GOAL_CONVERSION_BASE)
                       + GOAL_CONVERSION_GAIN * (quality - GOAL_CONVERSION_REF) )

GOAL_CONVERSION_REF is the league-average quality, so BASE is what an average attack
converts and GAIN is how much being the better team is worth. calibrate_sim.py reports the
observed league average so REF can be re-centred if the position weights ever change.

8. If a shot does not turn into a goal, OFFENSIVE_REBOUND_CHANCE of an offensive rebound.

9. Goalies: the starter plays until halftime, the backup opens the second half, and the
starter returns with BACKUP_GOALIE_MINUTES left. Whoever is in net is the goalie term in
rule 7, so backup quality genuinely changes goals conceded. Saves and goals allowed are
counted against the keeper who was actually facing that shot -- never apportioned by the
minutes schedule -- so a backup beaten repeatedly in his 15 minutes owns that line.

10. At the end of the game, final score will have been tabulated. Points will be given to individual players
consistent with their offensive contribution to the team. If the game is tied and ties are
not allowed, overtime is sudden death: alternating possessions, no clock, first goal wins.
